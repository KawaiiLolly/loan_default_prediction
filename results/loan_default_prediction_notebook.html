Loan Status Prediction¶

This notebook loads the loan dataset, cleans and preprocesses it, trains multiple classification models, tunes them with GridSearchCV, and exports the best model along with the encoders and scaler for later use in a Streamlit web app.

Reducing Skew with a Log Transform¶

ApplicantIncome and Total_Income are both strongly right-skewed, as seen in the outlier boxplots earlier. A log transform (log1p) pulls in the long tail and makes the distribution closer to normal, which tends to help linear and distance based models.

Key Insights from EDA¶

  • Class balance: about 68.7% of applications in this data were approved (Y) and 31.3% were not (N).
  • Credit history is the dominant signal: applicants with a credit history were approved 79.0% of the time, versus only 7.9% for those without one. This is by far the strongest relationship found in the data.
  • Property area matters moderately: Semiurban applicants had the highest approval rate (76.8%), ahead of Urban (65.8%) and Rural (61.5%).
  • Education has a smaller effect: Graduates were approved 70.8% of the time versus 61.2% for non-graduates.
  • Income alone is a weak predictor: mean Applicant Income was nearly identical between approved (5,384) and not-approved (5,446) applicants, and the same holds for Total Income (6,889 vs 7,324). Approval does not simply track how much an applicant earns.
  • Income and loan amount are heavily skewed: Applicant Income has a skew of 6.54 and Total Income 5.63; a log transform brings Total Income's skew down to 1.08, much closer to a normal distribution.
  • Outliers are present in ApplicantIncome, CoapplicantIncome, and LoanAmount, consistent with the skew above, and are worth keeping in mind for models sensitive to scale and extreme values.

In [39]:
results = []
best_estimators = {}

for name, mp in models_and_params.items():
    print(f"Training {name} ...")

    grid = GridSearchCV(
        estimator=mp["model"],
        param_grid=mp["params"],
        cv=5,
        scoring="accuracy",
        n_jobs=-1
    )
    grid.fit(X_train_scaled, y_train)

    best_model = grid.best_estimator_
    best_estimators[name] = best_model

    train_pred = best_model.predict(X_train_scaled)
    test_pred = best_model.predict(X_test_scaled)

    train_acc = accuracy_score(y_train, train_pred)
    test_acc = accuracy_score(y_test, test_pred)

    results.append({
        "Model": name,
        "Best Params": grid.best_params_,
        "Train Accuracy": train_acc,
        "Test Accuracy": test_acc
    })

    print(f"{name} done. Train Accuracy: {train_acc:.4f}, Test Accuracy: {test_acc:.4f}")
    print()
